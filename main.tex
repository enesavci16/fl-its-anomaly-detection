% ── Abstract ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) generate continuous streams of
sensor data that are conventionally aggregated at a centralised Traffic
Management Centre (TMC), posing significant privacy risks and bandwidth
demands.
In this paper, we propose a raw-data-locality-preserving traffic anomaly
detection framework based on Federated Learning (FL) and evaluate it on the
real-world PeMS04 dataset (307 sensors, 59 days, 5-minute intervals).
We emulate a four-node ITS topology using Mininet and the os-ken
OpenFlow~1.3 SDN controller, and implement the FedAvg aggregation
algorithm with an Isolation Forest (IF) anomaly detector.
A systematic three-scenario ablation study is conducted: (S1)~centralised
baseline, (S2)~IID-distributed FL, and (S3)~Non-IID FL with
heterogeneous sensor profiles.
Experimental results show that S2 achieves an average F1 score of
$0.741$ (multi-seed: $0.746 \pm 0.017$), only $4.0\%$ below the
centralised baseline ($0.772$), while keeping raw sensor data local.
S3 reveals a $28.4\%$ drop ($0.553$; multi-seed: $0.591 \pm 0.035$)
caused by client drift under heterogeneous data distributions.
A complementary evaluation confirms that Isolation Forest outperforms
Local Outlier Factor by a margin of $0.641$ F1 points on this dataset,
justifying the model selection \textit{a posteriori}.
Federated Threshold Calibration reduces total FL communication to
$1.78$~KB ($1.33$~KB client-to-server $+$ $0.45$~KB server-to-client
across all rounds), achieving an $\approx$11,800$\times$ reduction versus
the $\approx$21~MB raw-data transfer of the centralised baseline.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Modern ITS infrastructures rely on dense networks of IoT sensors---loop
detectors, speed radars, and cameras---that continuously monitor traffic
flow, speed, and occupancy.
Timely detection of anomalies such as accidents, congestion, or sensor
faults is critical for traffic management.
The dominant paradigm transmits raw sensor readings to a centralised TMC,
which introduces three fundamental vulnerabilities:
(i)~\textit{privacy risk}---vehicle trajectory data constitute personally
identifiable information regulated by frameworks such as GDPR and the
Turkish KVKK;
(ii)~\textit{bandwidth pressure}---hundreds of high-frequency sensors
saturate shared communication infrastructure;
(iii)~\textit{data island problem}---sensors owned by different
administrative entities cannot share raw data for joint model training
\cite{liu2021deep}.

Federated Learning (FL) \cite{mcmahan2017communication} addresses these
challenges by training models locally on each client and transmitting
only model parameters to a central aggregator, so raw data never leaves
the sensor node.
While FL has been applied to ITS traffic prediction
\cite{xia2023short,yuan2023fedSTN}, systematic ablation studies that
isolate the contribution of FL under realistic network conditions---using
actual SDN-controlled topologies and TCP sockets---remain scarce.

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We implement and evaluate FedAvg-based anomaly detection on a
        Mininet/os-ken SDN emulation with four heterogeneous PeMS04
        sensor nodes, providing a reproducible network-layer testbed.
  \item We conduct a three-scenario ablation study (S1~centralised,
        S2~IID FL, S3~Non-IID FL) that isolates the effects of
        federation and data heterogeneity on F1 score, communication
        cost, and convergence.
  \item We propose \textit{Federated Threshold Calibration}, a
        lightweight aggregation scheme for Isolation Forest that
        reduces total FL communication to $1.78$~KB across all
        rounds---an $\approx$11,800$\times$ reduction versus the
        centralised $\approx$21~MB raw-data transfer, verified via
        real TCP-socket byte counts in the Mininet emulation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{FL for ITS Applications}

McMahan et al.~\cite{mcmahan2017communication} introduced FedAvg, which
minimises communication cost by performing multiple local SGD steps
before aggregation.
Li et al.~\cite{li2020federated} systematically characterise the
challenges of statistical and system heterogeneity in FL, motivating our
Non-IID scenario.

In the ITS domain, Xia et al.~\cite{xia2023short} and
Yuan et al.~\cite{yuan2023fedSTN} apply graph-based FL to traffic flow
prediction on PeMS datasets, establishing benchmark performance figures
that we use for dataset validation.
Chellapandi et al.~\cite{chellapandi2024federated} survey FL for
connected vehicles, identifying communication cost and Non-IID data as
open challenges.

\subsection{FL for Anomaly Detection}

Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021deep} propose a communication-efficient
on-device FL framework for time-series anomaly detection in Industrial
IoT, which serves as the direct methodological template of this work.
Tham et al.~\cite{tham2023federated} compare FedAvg, FedProx, and
FedAvg-Adam for anomaly detection in vehicular networks under IID and
Non-IID settings, confirming that Non-IID distributions consistently
degrade global model performance.

\subsection{Research Gap}

Existing FL+ITS works predominantly simulate FL on a single machine
using frameworks such as Flower or PySyft, bypassing the network layer.
Furthermore, most FL+ITS studies focus on traffic \textit{prediction}
rather than anomaly \textit{detection}, and few conduct systematic
ablation studies isolating the Non-IID effect
\cite{zhang2024federated,kairouz2021advances}.
Client drift under heterogeneous distributions has been identified as
a fundamental challenge \cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}, and
FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} has been proposed as a remedy---both are
deferred to the journal extension of this work.
Sater and Hamza~\cite{sater2021federated} demonstrate FL-based anomaly
detection in SDN environments, yet rely on single-machine simulation
without a real network topology emulation.
Li et al.~\cite{li2025fedgdan} address Non-IID traffic prediction on
PeMS datasets with adaptive local aggregation, but do not consider
anomaly detection or network-layer emulation.

Our work explicitly emulates the communication topology with Mininet and
os-ken, measuring real TCP-socket byte counts and enabling future
integration of network impairment (delay, packet loss) without code
changes.

\section{System Design and Dataset}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Network Topology}

We emulate a four-node ITS network using Mininet~2.3.0
(Linux Bridge mode) and os-ken~2.3.1 as the OpenFlow~1.3 SDN
controller---the actively-maintained OpenStack fork of Ryu
\cite{osken2024}.
$K\!=\!4$ clients are selected as the minimum scale that captures
IID/Non-IID contrast while remaining within the memory budget of a
single-VM proof-of-concept deployment; scalability to larger
topologies is deferred to the journal extension.
Each host ($h_1$--$h_4$, IP 10.0.0.1--4) represents an ITS sensor
acting as an FL client; a fifth host (10.0.0.10) represents the TMC
aggregation server.
All hosts connect to a single OVS switch managed by os-ken via
\texttt{simple\_switch\_13}.
Connectivity is verified with \texttt{pingall} (0\% packet loss,
20/20 received); first-hop RTT~$\approx$1~ms (Packet-In + Flow-Mod
latency), subsequent RTT~$\approx$0.05~ms (flow-table hit).
FL messages are exchanged over TCP sockets with length-prefixed framing
(\texttt{struct.pack('>I', len)}) to handle large payloads safely.

\subsection{Dataset: PeMS04}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig06_selected_sensors_weekly.png}}
  \caption{Weekly traffic flow patterns of the four selected PeMS04
           sensors (P25/P50/P75/P95). Distinct diurnal and load profiles
           confirm Non-IID data heterogeneity across clients.}
  \label{fig:sensor_profiles}
\end{figure}

The PeMS04 dataset \cite{caltrans2024pems} comprises traffic
measurements from 307 sensors across 29 roads in the San Francisco Bay
Area over 59 days (January--February 2018) at 5-minute intervals,
yielding $16{,}992 \times 307 \times 3$ samples (flow, occupancy,
speed).\footnote{Column order as stored in the PeMS04 array:
flow, occupancy, speed. Feature selection in all experiments follows
this ordering.}
No missing values were found.

Four sensors are selected via a percentile-based strategy
(P25, P50, P75, P95 of mean flow) to ensure heterogeneous Non-IID
profiles representative of low-density rural, arterial, on-ramp, and
high-density highway segments, respectively
(sensor IDs: 278, 240, 71, 298).
A notable finding is that flow values never exceed $|z|>3$; anomalies
are therefore driven exclusively by occupancy and speed, which is
consistent with the distributional properties of PeMS data and is
reported as a dataset observation.

\subsection{Anomaly Labelling}

Anomaly labels are generated automatically using Z-score thresholding
($|z|>3$) applied per sensor per feature (OR rule: any feature
exceeding the threshold flags the time step as anomalous).
Z-score statistics ($\mu$, $\sigma$) are computed exclusively on the
training split to prevent data leakage.
A chronological 70/15/15 train/validation/test split is applied
(no shuffling) to preserve temporal integrity.
The resulting contamination rate across sensors ranges from
$1.49\%$ to $1.84\%$, confirming label consistency.

\section{Methodology}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Ablation Scenarios}

Three scenarios isolate the contribution of each system component:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{S1 --- Centralised Baseline:} All four sensors' data are
        pooled at the TMC\@. An Isolation Forest
        (\texttt{n\_estimators=100}, \texttt{max\_samples='auto'},
        \texttt{random\_state=42}) is trained on the combined dataset.
        The contamination hyperparameter is tuned on the validation
        split via grid search, yielding $c^*=0.00826$.
        Raw data ($\approx$21~MB, estimated) is transmitted to the TMC.
  \item \textbf{S2 --- IID FL:} Training data is shuffled and partitioned
        equally across four clients before being assigned to sensor nodes.
        FedAvg is executed over three communication rounds.
  \item \textbf{S3 --- Non-IID FL:} Each client retains its own sensor's
        natural traffic distribution (heterogeneous flow profiles),
        reflecting a realistic deployment where data islands are preserved.
\end{itemize}

For S2 and S3, each federated client uses the same contamination value
$c^*=0.00826$ obtained from the centralised grid search, ensuring a
controlled head-to-head comparison between S1 and the FL scenarios.

\subsection{Federated Threshold Calibration}

Standard FL aggregation of tree-based models (e.g., averaging decision
trees) is impractical due to structural incompatibility after
serialisation.
We propose \textit{Federated Threshold Calibration}: each client trains
a full local Isolation Forest and transmits only its anomaly decision
threshold (\texttt{offset\_}) to the TMC\@.
The server computes a sample-weighted average of client thresholds and
broadcasts the global threshold back to all clients, which update their
local decision boundaries without retraining.
This approach is directly aligned with the communication-efficient FL
philosophy of \cite{liu2021deep}.
Each client payload contains a single \texttt{float64} threshold and
sample count encoded as a JSON object ($\approx$113 bytes per client
per round), yielding a total client-to-server traffic of $1.33$~KB and
server-to-client traffic of $0.45$~KB across all rounds.
It is important to note that \textit{client drift} in this context
refers exclusively to \emph{threshold drift}: divergence of the
scalar \texttt{offset\_} values learned on heterogeneous local
distributions.
Tree structure is never transmitted or merged; only the scalar
threshold is federated.
This distinction decouples the aggregation mechanism from
sklearn's internal model representation and ensures correctness
after serialisation.

\subsection{FedAvg Protocol}

\begin{equation}
\hat{\tau}_{\text{global}} =
  \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_k \cdot \tau_k}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_k}
\label{eq:fedavg_threshold}
\end{equation}

\noindent
where $K=4$ is the number of clients, $n_k$ is the number of training
samples at client $k$, and $\tau_k$ is the local anomaly threshold
derived from the \texttt{offset\_} attribute of the locally trained
Isolation Forest.
The global threshold $\hat{\tau}_{\text{global}}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fedavg_threshold}) is then broadcast to all clients.
Three aggregation rounds are executed; all four clients participate in
every round (full participation).

Because Federated Threshold Calibration aggregates a single scalar per
client, the global threshold is fully determined after the first round;
subsequent rounds independently retrain local Isolation Forests with
different random seeds and re-aggregate, providing empirical
\emph{stability} evidence rather than iterative gradient-descent
refinement.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence}, the per-round mean F1 remains
stable for S2 ($0.742 \to 0.727 \to 0.713$ over 5 seeds) and similarly
for S3 ($0.587 \to 0.605 \to 0.589$), confirming that the Non-IID
performance bottleneck stems from distributional divergence rather than
insufficient rounds.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Performance is assessed on the held-out test set (15\% of each sensor's
data) using:
(1)~F1 score (primary metric, harmonic mean of Precision and Recall);
(2)~communication cost (total bytes transmitted over TCP sockets,
measured by the Mininet host processes);
(3)~client drift, defined as the signed difference between global and
local F1 per sensor
($\Delta_k = \text{F1}_{\text{global},k} - \text{F1}_{\text{local},k}$).

manifests as divergence between locally optimal and globally aggregated
thresholds; with heterogeneous Non-IID distributions, this divergence
is more pronounced, as each sensor's local threshold reflects its own
traffic regime.

\subsection{Communication Cost}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{fig12_comm_cost.png}}
  \caption{Communication cost comparison (log scale).
           S1 transmits $\approx$21~MB of raw sensor data (estimated)
           to the TMC, exposing vehicle trajectories.
           S2/S3 transmit only the scalar \texttt{offset\_} threshold
           ($\approx$113 bytes per client per round) upstream and the
           globally aggregated threshold downstream, achieving an
           $\approx$11,800$\times$ reduction in total communication
           versus S1.}
  \label{fig:commcost}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:commcost} and Table~\ref{tab:comm} compare the
communication overhead of the centralised and federated scenarios.
In the centralised architecture (S1), raw sensor measurements
($\approx$21~MB, estimated from per-sensor data volumes) are
transmitted to the TMC, introducing both significant bandwidth
consumption and inherent privacy risk through the exposure of
individual vehicle trajectories.

In the federated scenarios (S2/S3), Federated Threshold Calibration
eliminates raw-data transmission entirely.
Each client transmits only its local anomaly threshold
(\texttt{offset\_})---a single \texttt{float64} value embedded in a
length-prefixed JSON payload of $\approx$113 bytes per client per
round, measured directly from TCP-socket byte counts in the Mininet
emulation.
Across four clients and three rounds, client-to-server traffic totals
$1.33$~KB\@.
The server broadcasts the globally aggregated threshold back to all
clients at $0.15$~KB per round ($0.45$~KB total).
The combined FL communication footprint is therefore $\mathbf{1.78}$~\textbf{KB}---an
$\approx$11,800$\times$ reduction versus the centralised baseline.
The FL byte counts are verified via real TCP-socket measurements in the
Mininet emulation; the $\approx$21~MB centralised reference is an
estimate of raw sensor data volume and is not directly instrumented.

Raw sensor data never leaves any client node, ensuring \textit{raw data
locality}: no vehicle trajectories are exposed to the TMC or to other
clients.

\section{Discussion}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\textbf{Privacy--accuracy trade-off (AS1):}
S2 demonstrates that FL achieves $96.0\%$ of the centralised F1
without ever transmitting raw sensor data, directly answering AS1.
The $4.0\%$ gap is attributable to the single-round aggregation of
scalar thresholds rather than iterative gradient exchange.
A one-sample $t$-test confirms that the S2 F1 distribution
($0.746 \pm 0.017$) is statistically significantly below S1
($t=-3.433$, $p=0.027$), yet the practical effect size remains modest
($\Delta=-0.025$), supporting the acceptability of this trade-off for
privacy-sensitive deployments.

\textbf{Communication cost (AS2):}
Total FL communication ($1.78$~KB across all rounds) is
$\approx$11,800$\times$ lower than the $\approx$21~MB centralised
raw-data transfer, directly validating FL's communication advantage
(AS2).
Client-to-server traffic is reduced to $1.33$~KB by transmitting only
the scalar \texttt{offset\_} threshold ($\approx$113 bytes per client
per round) rather than the full Isolation Forest model;
server-to-client traffic amounts to $0.45$~KB for broadcasting the
globally aggregated threshold.
The FL byte counts are measured from actual TCP-socket counts in the
Mininet emulation; the $\approx$21~MB centralised figure is an estimate
of raw sensor data volume.
The privacy implications of the scalar threshold (e.g., membership
inference from \texttt{offset\_}) are beyond the scope of this
proof-of-concept; Differential Privacy mechanisms will be evaluated in
the journal extension.

\textbf{Non-IID effect and client drift (AS3):}
The $15.5$~percentage-point gap between S2 and S3 confirms that data
heterogeneity is the dominant factor degrading FL performance (AS3).
A paired $t$-test over 5 seeds confirms this gap is statistically
significant ($t=10.270$, $p=0.0005$), and a one-sample test confirms
S3 is significantly below S1 ($t=-11.479$, $p=0.0003$).
Per-sensor drift analysis (Table~\ref{tab:persensor}) reveals
pronounced divergence under Non-IID: sensor~240 (P50) suffers a
$-0.133$ threshold drift, while sensor~298 (P95) benefits by $+0.197$,
illustrating that the global threshold is a compromise that favours
high-density sensors.
The weighted-average aggregation of FedAvg is insufficient to reconcile
divergent threshold distributions; adaptive aggregation strategies
(e.g., FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox},
SCAFFOLD~\cite{karimireddy2020scaffold}, personalised FL) are reserved
for the journal extension.

\textbf{Algorithm selection --- IF vs.\ LOF (AS1 supplement):}
To justify the choice of Isolation Forest over alternative unsupervised
detectors, we additionally evaluate Local Outlier Factor (LOF)
\cite{breunig2000lof} under the same centralised pooled setting and
feature ordering (\texttt{n\_train=47,576}, $c^*=0.00826$,
features: flow, occupancy, speed).
LOF assigns anomaly scores based on the ratio of a point's local
reachability density to that of its $k$ nearest neighbours: points
residing in sparse neighbourhoods relative to their surroundings
receive high outlier scores.
While effective in low-dimensional, small-scale datasets, LOF exhibits
three structural weaknesses on PeMS04.
\textit{First}, the pooled training set (47,576 samples) makes
neighbourhood computation statistically unstable, as local density
estimates lose discriminative power at this scale.
\textit{Second}, PeMS04 features span vastly different ranges---flow
($0$--$919$), occupancy ($0$--$0.77$), speed ($3$--$85$~mph)---causing
Euclidean-distance-based neighbourhood search to be dominated by the
flow dimension; even after StandardScaler normalisation, F1 remains
below $0.14$ across all tested values of $k \in \{20,50,100,200\}$.
\textit{Third}, anomalies in this dataset are rare ($\approx$2.85\%)
and manifest as isolated temporal spikes rather than coherent spatial
clusters, which structurally disadvantages density-based methods.
Isolation Forest, by contrast, exploits the same sparsity property
directly: anomalies are isolated with fewer random partitions
regardless of feature scale.
The resulting F1 gap (IF: $0.772$ vs.\ LOF: $0.131$, margin $0.641$)
confirms that isolation-based detection is the appropriate choice for
large-scale ITS sensor data, and validates our model selection
\textit{a posteriori}.

\textbf{Limitations:}
(i)~Isolation Forest threshold aggregation is a simplification of true
model federation; deep learning models would enable gradient-based
FedAvg.
(ii)~The emulation does not introduce artificial network impairments
(delay, jitter, packet loss); this is planned for the Q1 journal version.
(iii)~Only one benchmark dataset (PeMS04) is used; cross-validation on
PeMS08 and METR-LA is deferred.

\section{Conclusion}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We presented a raw-data-locality-preserving FL framework for traffic
anomaly detection, evaluated on PeMS04 via a Mininet/os-ken SDN
emulation.
Our three-scenario ablation study shows that IID FL achieves $96.0\%$
of centralised accuracy while keeping raw data local; the residual
$4.0\%$ gap is attributable to single-round scalar threshold aggregation
rather than full gradient exchange, and its precise decomposition is left
as future work.
Federated Threshold Calibration reduces total FL communication to
$1.78$~KB across all rounds---an $\approx$11,800$\times$ reduction versus
the centralised $\approx$21~MB raw-data transfer, verified via real
TCP-socket byte counts in the Mininet emulation.
These findings provide a reproducible, network-layer baseline for future
FL+ITS research.

Future work will extend the framework with FedProx under realistic
4G/LTE channel models, Differential Privacy, and gradient compression,
targeting further reductions in the $4.0\%$ accuracy gap and enabling
scalability beyond the current four-client proof-of-concept topology.
